# Equipment and safety

**Read this before the session, and before volunteering.** Applies to both
electrical-stimulation blocks.

The TMS block has its own separate protocol and screening.

## Kit, per group of 3–4

| Item | Qty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Two-channel TENS/EMS stimulator | 1 | Must have a **monophasic rectangular** mode (Block A) and two independently programmable kHz channels (Block B). Sessions built around a TENS Ultima Neo. |
| Self-adhesive electrodes, 3 cm circular | 4 per volunteer | Preferred over 5 × 5 cm: thresholds ~30% lower, so you stay clear of the device's voltage ceiling at short pulse widths. |
| Self-adhesive electrodes, 5 × 5 cm | 2 spare | For the electrode-size comparison, and as backup. |
| Conductive gel | 1 tube | Small pads dry out fast; re-gel between volunteers. |
| Alcohol wipes | pack | Skin prep. Let it dry before applying electrodes. |
| Skin marker | 1 | Marking the electrode outline is not optional — see below. |
| Ruler, 15 cm, rigid | 1 | Stood on edge, as the pointer scale. |
| Drinking straw + medical tape | 1 | Taped to the thumb, it turns a 2 mm twitch into a 2 cm excursion. |
| Blunt plastic probe | 1 | Block B, cutaneous-afferent testing. Never anything sharp. |
| Stopwatch or phone timer | 1 | Counting contractions over 10 s. |
| 1 kΩ resistor (¼ W is fine) | 1 | Device compliance test. |
| Multimeter | 1 per room | Lead-polarity test. |
| Laptop | 1 | Colab, or a local clone. |

Room-level: a printed copy of this file at the instructor table, and spare electrodes — students will peel and re-stick more than you expect, and a
half-adhered pad concentrates current at its edge.

## Exclusion criteria — ask every volunteer, every block

Do **not** stimulate anyone who:

- has a pacemaker, ICD, neurostimulator, cochlear implant or any other implanted electronic device;
- has a seizure disorder or a history of seizures;
- has a known cardiac arrhythmia;
- has broken, inflamed or infected skin, or a skin condition, at the electrode site;
- is pregnant or may be pregnant;
- has a peripheral neuropathy, or altered sensation in the arm;
- has a recent fracture, surgery or metal hardware in the arm;
- does not want to. No reason required, no follow-up question, no group pressure.

A volunteer may withdraw at any moment without explaining. Say this out loud before asking for
volunteers, so that declining is visibly normal.

## Consent script

> "This session uses a small battery-powered nerve stimulator on one forearm. Most people describe
> it as tingling, then a twitch of the thumb or fingers. It is uncomfortable at the higher settings
> and it should never be painful. We will start at zero and go up slowly, in small steps, and we
> stop at the first clear twitch — we are not interested in how strong it can get. You can say stop
> at any point and we will take the current to zero immediately, no questions. You can also say no
> now, or change your mind halfway through. Do any of the things on this exclusion list apply to
> you?" *(read the list)* "Do you have any questions before we start?"

Record verbal consent on the group's handout: volunteer initials, date, instructor initials.

## Rules while stimulating

1. **Start at 0 mA. Always.** Ramp about 1 mA every 2–3 s, and talk to the volunteer at each step.
2. **Stop at the endpoint you came for.** For Block A that is the 5-of-10 motor threshold; for
   Block B a comfortable pulsatile contraction. Do not go past it.
3. **One forearm only.** Never across the chest, the neck, the head, or from one arm to the other.
   Never across the heart.
4. **Both electrodes firmly attached before the amplitude leaves zero,** and back to zero before
   anything is peeled, moved or re-gelled. A partially lifted electrode concentrates the whole
   current into its remaining contact area.
5. **Total time under stimulation stays short** — a few minutes per volunteer. Block B's afferent
   block asks for ~60 s of continuous kHz current; that is the longest continuous exposure in the
   session and it is deliberate.
6. **Inspect the skin** when electrodes come off, and again at the break. In Block A's monophasic
   mode look under the **cathode** first: the medium goes alkaline there and erythema appears there
   before anywhere else.
7. **Fresh electrodes per volunteer.** Never shared between people.
8. **Stop immediately** on any report of pain, burning, sharpness, dizziness or unease. Amplitude to
   zero first, then talk.
9. A facilitator confirms every montage before the first pulse.

## About monophasic mode specifically

Block A uses monophasic pulses, which carry a net direct current — the reason clinical devices
default to biphasic. At the settings used here it is negligible: 20 mA × 200 µs at 1 Hz is a 4 µA
average, about 0.6 µA/cm² under a 3 cm disc, four orders of magnitude below any irritation
threshold. The two rules that follow from it are in the list above (item 5, and cathode-first skin
inspection at item 6). Do not leave a monophasic output running at high frequency.

## Incident handling

Any skin reaction that does not fade within a few minutes, any report of pain that persists after
the current stops, or any unexpected sensation elsewhere in the body: stop the group, note what was
set on the device and where the electrodes were, and tell the room lead. Write it down while it is
fresh — including the settings, which is the part everyone forgets.
